# Landing page sections

Section-by-section rules for website and landing page copy. Use them after the messaging building blocks are confirmed. Every section should reinforce the hero's core claim.

## Hero section

**Purpose:** communicate the top-level messaging building blocks (problem, product, audience, and differentiation) in the first viewport.

**Header:**
- Must be fully descriptive. A visitor who reads only this should know what the product is.
- Include at least two of: target audience, product category, problem, and functional benefit.
- Add a hook: either a bold, specific claim or an objection addressed head-on.
- Formula: `[What the product does or core benefit]. [Objection crusher or specific proof.]`
- Keep it under 12 words.

**Subheader:**
- One to two sentences at most.
- Expand on what the product is, and on which features or capabilities make the header's claim credible.

**CTA:**
- Action-oriented language that continues the hero's narrative.
- It must feel like the natural next step. "Start deploying" after a developer hero is natural. "Learn more" after a vague hero is weak.

**Rationale format:** after writing the hero, state which positioning anchor you used, what competitive alternative is implied, and why you led with the chosen element (problem, category, use case, or benefit).

### Worked example

Fictional product: a shared inbox for property management teams, replacing a group Gmail account.

- Weak: "The powerful platform for modern teams." It has no anchor, no audience, and a vague qualifier.
- Strong: "The shared inbox for property managers. No more lost tenant emails."
  - Anchor: product category (shared inbox), with an audience secondary anchor.
  - Implied alternative: a group Gmail account where requests slip through.
  - Hook: the objection crusher names the pain of the alternative.

## Social proof section

- The heading describes who the customers are. "Trusted by 500+ B2B sales teams" beats "Companies love us".
- Logos build breadth. Quotes build depth. Use both when available.

## Benefits sections (two to three)

- Each benefit header states a pain-focused value proposition in plain, specific language.
- The paragraph explains the benefit and can handle one key objection.
- Include proof points: features, stats, or customer outcomes.
- Include a product screenshot where possible.
- Together, the benefits should differentiate the product from competitors.
- Keep narrative continuity with the hero.

## Use case or persona section

- Two to three entries.
- Each describes the activity or role and how the product helps.
- For horizontal products, segment by persona. For vertical products, segment by use case.

## How it works section

- Lead with a short description of the product.
- Three clear steps.
- Use a diagram or annotated visual if the user can provide one.

## CTA bar (above the footer)

- Restate the core value proposition.
- Create urgency or give a reason to act now.
- Repeat the primary CTA.

## Features and objections sections

- Each block has a header (value proposition), a paragraph (explanation and objection handling), and an image.
- The best sections carry a running narrative that ties each feature back to the hero's core claim.
