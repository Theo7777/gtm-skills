# Coding guide for desk research

## Search strings

Replace `[category]`, `[tool]`, and `[problem]` with the problem space.

- `site:reddit.com "[tool]" alternative`
- `site:reddit.com "[problem]" "how do you"`
- `site:reddit.com "switched from [tool]"`
- `"[tool]" review "cons"` (then open G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot pages)
- `"[category]" "I hate" OR "so frustrating" OR "gave up"`
- `"[tool]" "cancelled" OR "churned" OR "moved to"`
- App Store and Play Store: sort reviews by most recent and by lowest rating

Good venues beyond Reddit: niche Slack or Discord communities with public archives, Indie Hackers, Hacker News, Quora, industry forums, and YouTube comment threads on tool reviews.

## Coding sheet

Log one row per useful source before clustering.

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Source | URL, venue, date |
| Situation | What was happening when they posted ("When I...") |
| Job | What they are trying to get done |
| Pain | The struggle, in their words |
| Tool | Current tool or workaround named |
| Switching moment | Any sign they are leaving, joining, or stuck |
| Force | Push, pull, anxiety, habit, or none |
| Quote | Verbatim line worth keeping |

## Strength rating

- **Strong**: five or more independent sources across two or more venues
- **Moderate**: three or four independent sources
- **Thin**: one or two sources, or all from a single community

## Red flags in evidence

- Reviews that read like marketing copy, or clusters posted on the same day
- Threads older than three years in a fast-moving category
- A single power user dominating a thread
- Complaints about pricing from people who never paid
