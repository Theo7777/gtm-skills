# Interview question bank

Use this when the user wants to run their own switch interviews. Talk to people who recently switched to (or away from) a solution, ideally within the last 90 days, so the memory is fresh. Aim for 30 to 45 minutes and at least six interviews before drawing conclusions.

The structure follows the timeline of a purchase: first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, and first use.

## Set the scene

- Take me back to when you first started using [product]. When was that?
- What were you using before? How long had you used it?

## First thought

- When did you first realise the old way was not working?
- What was happening that day? Where were you, and who was involved?
- What had changed in your work or life around then?

## Passive looking (push)

- What did you try to fix it before looking for something new?
- What was the cost of doing nothing?

## Active looking (pull)

- What made you actually start searching? What was the trigger that day?
- Where did you look first? Who did you ask?
- What else did you consider, including hiring someone or building it yourself?
- What did you picture life would be like once this was solved?

## Deciding (anxiety and habit)

- What nearly stopped you from switching?
- What were you worried might go wrong?
- What did you like about the old way that you were reluctant to lose?
- Who else had to agree? What did they need to hear?

## First use

- What happened the first time you used it?
- When did you know it was working, or not?
- What would make you switch again?

## Interview rules

- Ask for stories, not opinions. "Tell me about the last time" beats "Do you like".
- Dig into specifics: dates, places, people, and money.
- Stay quiet after answers; the useful detail often comes second.
- Never pitch or defend the product during the interview.
- Record with consent, and remove names before sharing transcripts.
